FICTIONAL CARD DISPUTE CASE: CB-81
All names, case references, events, dates and amounts are invented.
This is a card-dispute training example, not a UPI failed-transaction case.

QUESTION: Can the associate conclude that the merchant wins the case?
ANSWER: No. The order status and one access event do not by themselves
prove delivery under the applicable card-network reason and evidence
rules. The pending refund is not a posted refund. The analyst also
cannot promise the cardholder a reversal from these records.

MODEL EVIDENCE REGISTER
- Notice DN-81: allegation, amount and case status; confirm applicable
  reason, response channel and deadline from the acquirer/provider.
- Order OR-81: completed status; locate underlying service terms and
  independent delivery or usage evidence before describing fulfilment.
- Service log SV-81: one access event; verify whose event it was and
  whether it is relevant under the applicable rule. Minimise personal data.
- Refund queue RF-81: request pending. Check whether any refund actually
  posted, and prevent duplicate action through the authorised workflow.
- Settlement ST-81: original transaction settled. Track the dispute and
  any later reversal separately in reconciliation.

MODEL CASE NOTE
CB-81, INR 1,200: dispute notice alleges service not received. Order
system shows completed and service log records one access event, but
delivery relevance is unverified. Refund request pending; no posted
refund evidence in this packet. Status: open. Send source IDs and
timeline to the authorised dispute reviewer; confirm network reason,
deadline and permitted evidence with the acquirer. Reconcile any later
refund/reversal after it is confirmed. No outcome promised.

SELF-CHECK: identify the notice and deadline, distinguish status from
proof, distinguish request from posted refund, avoid unsupported outcome,
protect data, and name reviewer plus reconciliation step. One point
each; six points is a learning aid only.
